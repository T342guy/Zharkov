
Zharkov is T342guy's very own ConLang (Composed language) heavily based off of english.

## Linguistics Basics

### Alphabet & Orthography

Zharkov uses the standard 26 English letters.

### Vowels

Zharkov uses the five basic English vowels, but with a twist for that harsh feel:

- **a** — short: like "cat" `kæt`; long **ā** — deep and back: like "father" but stretched and throaty `ɑ`
- **e** — short: like "bed" `ɛ`; long **ē** — like "say" without the glide `e`
- **i** — short: like "bit" `ɪ`; long **ī** — like "machine" `i`
- **o** — short: like "core" `ɔ`; long **ō** — deep "oh" `o`
- **u** — short: like "book" `ʊ`; long **ū** — like "rule" but drawn out `u`

### Consonants & Special Sounds

Most consonants are English-like, but Zharkov loves clusters and throat action:

- **kh** — harsh velar/uvular fricative (German "Bach", Scottish "loch", Russian х)
- **gh** — voiced raspy version (throaty gargle)
- **r** — uvular or trilled (growly back-of-throat r, like French/German "r" or rolled Russian р)
- **sh** — "sh" in "ship"
- **zh** — "s" in "measure"
- **ch** — "ch" in "church"
- Heavy clusters allowed: thr-, kr-, pht-, shk-, zhr-, kht-, etc.

### Phonotactics (Sound Rules)

- Syllable structure: C.V.O. (Based off of english)
- No soft, flowy sounds — avoid too many liquids (l, w) or glides in a row.
- Stress usually on the first syllable, emphasizing clusters and long vowels.

### Language Rules (Making new words)

- No one word in Zharkov may have 2 meanings based on context. (this is what english does btw)
