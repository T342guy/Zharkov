**Zharkov** is a personal constructed language (conlang) designed for fun, daily practice, and eventual fluency. It draws heavily on English roots for vocabulary and spelling to make it easier for English speakers to pick up, while delivering a deliberately harsh, raspy, guttural sound inspired by Russian and German.

## Linguistics Basics

### Alphabet & Orthography

Zharkov uses the standard 26 English letters.\
Long vowels are marked with a macron (¯) above the letter: **ā ē ī ō ū**.\
These are easy to type (long-press on phones, alt codes on Windows, or copy-paste).

### Vowels

Zharkov uses the five basic English vowels, but with a twist for that harsh feel:

- **a** — short: like "cat" /kæt/; long **ā** — deep and back: like "father" but stretched and throaty /ɑː/
- **e** — short: like "bed" /ɛ/; long **ē** — like "say" without the glide /eː/
- **i** — short: like "bit" /ɪ/; long **ī** — like "machine" /iː/
- **o** — short: like "core" /ɔ/; long **ō** — deep "oh" /oː/
- **u** — short: like "book" /ʊ/; long **ū** — like "rule" but drawn out /uː/

**Key rule**: A, O, and U are **usually long and deep** (ā, ō, ū) in stressed syllables for that guttural growl. Short versions appear in unstressed or quick words.

### Consonants & Special Sounds

Most consonants are English-like, but Zharkov loves clusters and throat action:

- **kh** — harsh velar/uvular fricative (German "Bach", Scottish "loch", Russian х) — **/x/** or **/χ/**
- **gh** — voiced raspy version (throaty gargle) — **/ɣ/** or **/ʁ/**
- **r** — uvular or trilled (growly back-of-throat r, like French/German "r" or rolled Russian р) — **/ʁ/** or **/r/**
- **sh** — "sh" in "ship" — **/ʃ/**
- **zh** — "s" in "measure" — **/ʒ/**
- **ch** — "ch" in "church" — **/tʃ/**
- Heavy clusters allowed: thr-, kr-, pht-, shk-, zhr-, kht-, etc. — smash them aggressively!

**R is deepened and elongated** in most positions — think **R**ight but from the back of the throat, rolled or gargled for extra rasp.

### Phonotactics (Sound Rules)

- Syllable structure: (C)(C)(C)V(C)(C) — lots of initial and final clusters for harshness.
- No soft, flowy sounds — avoid too many liquids (l, w) or glides in a row.
- Stress usually on the first syllable, emphasizing clusters and long vowels.

## Quick Example

English: "I see the big fire in the house."\
Zharkov: **Gak khtōr da zarkh krāg in da shkva.**

Say it with throat rasp on *kh*, growl the *r*, stretch *ō* and *ā* — it should feel aggressive and deep.
